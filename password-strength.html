Why there is no character-type checklist

NIST SP 800-63B-4, section 3.1.1 tells password verifiers to require at least 15 characters for single-factor passwords. Passwords used only as part of multi-factor authentication may be shorter, but must have at least 8 characters. These are verifier requirements, not guarantees that a sample is secure.

NIST says verifiers must not require a mix of uppercase, lowercase, digits, or symbols. They must check the whole proposed password against an appropriate blocklist of common, expected, or compromised passwords. The tiny teaching checks here do not perform that full check.

Length helps, but predictable choices, reuse, phishing, and a service's protections also matter. Passwords themselves are not phishing-resistant.
